What is an AI charter?

A few sentences that decide how an AI answers you

When you ask an AI model a question, how it answers is not an accident. Developers train it, after its basic training, to follow a set of principles: be honest, avoid harm, respect people’s choices. Some companies call this a constitution. For Apertus, built by the Swiss AI Initiative, it is the Apertus Charter.

The charter is used as the example the model learns from and as the yardstick for judging its answers. So a few words in it can decide whether the AI answers a question, refuses, or sends you somewhere else.

Principles sound easy to agree on. The hard part is what they mean in a real situation, and that is where people’s views differ.

Article 4 · Preventing harm

4.3Harmful requests must be refused, even if they sound legitimate.

Which household chemicals should never be mixed? Under a strict readingI can’t help with combining chemicals. If the clause said “refused only when the harm is clear”Never mix bleach with ammonia or with acids such as vinegar: both release toxic gases. Store them apart.

Clause shortened from the Apertus Charter, Version 1.0. The two answers are illustrations, not real Apertus output.

Why your input matters

The people an AI serves should have a say in its values

  1. 1It affects everyone who uses it

    An AI model answers millions of questions about health, money, school and work. Its charter quietly decides what it will and won’t help with.

  2. 2A small team can’t foresee everything

    The Apertus Charter promises “participatory refinement”, but there is no way to take part yet. Different people notice different problems; your everyday situations are what the charter is missing.

  3. 3Your choices can train a model

    Your ratings and examples are turned into test cases. They show whether a model trained on the new charter actually behaves differently.

What happens with your input

From your answers to a model you can check

  1. 1 · CollectRatings, comparisons, proposalsEveryone’s rating counts the same. Answers are private; only totals are shown.
  2. 2 · DecideBroad supportA change is endorsed when clearly more people find it helpful than not. Disagreement stays visible.
  3. 3 · TrainA new charter versionWe fine-tune a small copy of Apertus on the endorsed changes and their test cases.
  4. 4 · CheckA public scorecardBefore and after, on cases the model never saw. Anyone can see whether it changed.

Good to know

What this is, and what it isn’t

This is a student research project in AI and Human Values at ETH Zurich. It is not run by or affiliated with the Swiss AI Initiative, and your input does not change the official Apertus model.

Until enough real input has come in, the results page shows a sample round with made-up participants, clearly labelled. Your own answers are added on top.

We ask for as little as possible: an email address to sign in, and three optional questions (age group, language, how often you use AI) so the results can show whether groups see things differently.

For organisations

Run this for your own AI and community

A hospital, a school or a canton can run the same process for its own charter and its own open model, on its own server.
